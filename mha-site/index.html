What did Europe look like when the first megaliths were built?
Who were the people who raised them — and why?

The Megalithic Heritage Atlas takes you back thousands of years, to a Europe emerging from the last Ice Age. Explore a changing continent of vanished coastlines, ancient forests, rivers and lakes, and follow the transformation from hunter-gatherer societies to the first farming communities and the builders of Europe's megaliths.

More than 35,000 megalithic monuments are known across Europe alone. Many more exist in the world. Yet their origins, connections and meaning remain one of archaeology's enduring mysteries.

Where did the tradition begin? How did it spread across the continent — and beyond? And what connects megaliths built thousands of kilometres apart?

The atlas brings together different pieces of the puzzle:

Ancient landscapes

Geology, coastlines, rivers and lost lands such as Doggerland.

Megalithic monuments

Their locations, ages and architectural traditions.

People and cultures

Archaeology, ancient DNA and changing populations.

A changing planet

Climate change, floods, tsunamis and volcanic events.

By placing these datasets on the same map, the Megalithic Heritage Atlas reveals connections that are difficult to see when each story is studied on its own.

This is more than a map of ancient monuments. It is an invitation to explore the world in which they were built — and to discover how landscape, people and climate shaped one another.
